Make crawlers earn their power bill.

SECTION 01

Understand what you are buying

Crawlers are stationary, vulnerable production support. Their construction prerequisites, unit cost and class requirement belong in the project budget. Begin with reliable mines and power, and do not treat them as ships that can accompany a fleetsave.

Details and worked examples →
SECTION 02

Calculate the working limit

Keep owned count, permitted working count and realized production separate. Mine levels set baseline working capacity; Collector, Geologist and Lifeform effects can change the result. The documented 50% production-cap repair is July 2026 PTS evidence, not a new live-universe measurement.

Details and worked examples →
SECTION 03

Work through one planet

The 30/25/25 mine example separates 640 base working crawlers from 704 with baseline Collector and Geologist capacity. Its comparison table excludes Lifeform effects and shows the different production and energy consequences of Collector overload.

Details and worked examples →
SECTION 04

Stop before extra spending stops helping

A working-count increase need not increase income at a production ceiling. Do not buy or power a batch that produces no useful extra output. The exact complete Lifeform crawler interaction remains unverified, so check the account’s actual bonus row.

Details and worked examples →
SECTION 05

Measure the marginal return

Overload increases energy demand faster than crawler output. Compare additional Metal, Crystal and Deuterium retained after supplying that power, including additional fusion fuel. The reference provides six workload rows and a marginal net-income example.

Details and worked examples →
SECTION 06

Add crawlers in verified batches

Verify one planet before repeating an investment. Check count, energy and final output at the intended workload. Recalculate after mine upgrades, class changes, Geologist expiry or Lifeform changes, and keep a replacement and power-recovery reserve.
