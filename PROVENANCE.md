## Editorial structure

The site is a topic-based guide and reference manual, not a tutorial course or recruitment landing page. Concise overviews lead to separately linked detailed reference pages. Tables identify targets, units, assumptions and version limits; deeper references remain searchable without crowding the core navigation. The design retains in-page navigation principles from:

- USWDS in-page navigation: readable long pages with a stable contents menu. https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/in-page-navigation/
- W3C G64: contents links that match the sequence and targets of the document. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G64

## Mechanics and calculations

Each guide lists its sources. Official administrator guides from October 2026 support expedition and colonization details. Older official forum references support long-standing building, research, energy and fleet-safety mechanics; their age is disclosed. Community explanations are identified rather than silently treated as official announcements.

Energy calculations use the published unmodified Solar Plant, Fusion Reactor and Energy Technology formulas. The table is full-output and no-bonus; fuel is shown at economy ×1 and ×8. Energy is rounded down and fuel up for display; live rounding can differ by one unit. The comparison is not an equal-cost analysis. Technology investment, operating fuel, satellite vulnerability, temperature and fields are all part of the decision.

Crawler examples separate owned count, allowed working count and production ceiling. Worked rows exclude Lifeform modifiers. Collector Enhancement can change the working-capacity bonus. The 50% production-cap reference includes the official July 2026 PTS changelog and is not presented as a newly measured live-universe limit. A third-party calculator discussion with untested cap assumptions was not used as validation.

Colony tables identify base field ranges, maximum-temperature ranges and position bonuses. They exclude class, universe, item and Lifeform field adjustments. Position bonuses act on the production base before subsequent production modifiers. The fields example uses the official 2026 colonization guide rather than multiplying rounded field values.

General planning guides were reconciled against earlier user discussions, but earlier assistant calculations were not treated as authoritative. Only generalizable concepts were retained. Private account numbers, coordinates, planet names, screenshots, schedules and conversation links are not published.

## Orion preparation and evidence boundaries

The Orion guide now gives a conditional merge-day opening plan: confirm activation and live prerequisites; build low-level scanners across usable planets; fund a modest conversion workload and a claim reserve; complete an affordable qualifying mission; then advance toward a useful checkpoint. The recommendations are planning guidance, not a claim that a particular merge target is active. The official US merge announcement identifies the targets but does not supply that flag. The version 13.1.0 notice describing 6 November activation and Gameforge's planned 30 November global rollout are reported within their respective scope.

Official direction that IAS affects anomaly level, rewards and difficulty comes from the Project Orion introduction and FAQ. No verified function from total IAS to exact reward, current prerequisite list, or daily mission cap was found. The guide does not invent them. Exact mission levels, reward cards, deadlines, delivery destinations and Techinfo remain the checks for actual play. Later patches supersede stale FAQ details.

The historical evidence is pinned to Project Orion guide 0.19.6, 11 October 2026, commit `56a951bbeca850d00ff1ccca106b05475daa7fb3`, describing PTS v13.1.0 beta13. The 50–350 completed-mission unlock table is labelled as that snapshot, not a current specification for building effects. The Lithium Lab and Anomaly Analysis Center especially require a current effect check. The original Orion project remains unchanged.

The IAS cost model uses exact rational 3/2 scaling, with Metal 84, Crystal 42 and Deuterium 14 bases, and each component floored at each level. The 15-planet table sums that model across a hypothetical balanced network. It excludes prerequisites and other setup costs. Equal exponential per-planet costs justify balancing for a total-access target; this does not optimize Lithium throughput or imply equal output across different layouts. The resource columns are separate units, not a value-equivalence total.

The reward table now uses five visually verified mission-summary cards from 28 October 2026, PTS 13.1.0-beta18. Every displayed resource component carries an approximation symbol. The raw M + C + D sums are approximately 670,450,730 at mission 617; 1,415,430,447 at 658; 607,756,824 at 748; 637,096,077 at 766; and 605,341,038 at 772. These are possible per-mission amounts, not wave claims or collected totals. The separate collected-reward area was empty. Rounded Lithium Reward Costs are copied as displayed, not claimed to be paid or independently verified full liabilities. Ships and other rewards are not valued in the sums.

The level-658 card is paired with separately reported total IAS 693. That IAS value was not visible in the image and is not extended to the other four records. Every selected scan level remains unknown. Only the mechanical values, observation date and game version are retained publicly. The original images, private source IDs, conversation links, coordinates, balances and account details are excluded.

The selected screenshots are not a random or representative sample. Their mission templates, stars and waves differ, while class and other bonus context is incomplete. The approximately 605 million–1.415 billion span describes only these cards; it is not a confidence interval, expected return, ceiling, guaranteed minimum or current live forecast. Older October 1–3 observations are not pooled with the beta18 cards. No global reward curve is fitted.

For the level mapping, T denotes total IAS, S the selected scan level and M the discovered mission level. Official-forum guidance supports selecting a lower level and variation of about ±20% around S. At maximum S = T, the approximate mission interval is 0.8T–1.2T; at T = 693, that is 554.4–831.6 before undocumented rounding. Mission 658 is compatible, about 5.05% below 693, but the maximum-setting assumption is not recorded. Inverting the rule gives S between M / 1.2 and M / 0.8, not M ±20%. At M = 658 this is about 548.33–822.5 if total IAS is unknown; with reported T = 693 the compatible setting interval narrows to about 548.33–693. These are conditional mathematical intervals, not recovered scan settings or a reward formula.

The beta15 storage limit, beta16 chance-Lithium reward change and beta17 ship-reward rebalance precede the historical calculator. No universal Lithium-income calculator is included. Private account coordinates, planet names, fleets, balances, screenshots and conversation links are not published.
