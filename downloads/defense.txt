Defense planning
Skie Field Manual · Evidence reviewed 9 November 2026. Read the linked reference for formulas, sources and version limits; current account settings remain the final check.

This guide covers:
- Set a defensive budget from your collection interval and realistic local threats.
- Choose units for a job instead of copying a universal defense ratio.
- Separate combat losses, repaired units and permanent attrition.
- Check missile protection, combat bonuses and debris settings before trusting a simulation.

Make the routine raid unattractive

Defense should make attacks on the resources and ships you normally leave behind unattractive. It is not a substitute for fleetsaving. Start with the likely attacker, exposed value and the amount of permanent loss you can afford.

Read the universe before choosing a build

Debris rules, repair settings, fleet speeds and current bonuses change the value of the same defense. Write these assumptions beside the comparison. Advice from another universe can give the wrong answer even with an identical unit mix.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#universe

Give each part of the defense a job

Cheap targets, larger guns and shield domes serve different roles. Their useful mix depends on the attacking fleet, technologies and budgets. The detailed unit-role table supports a comparison, not a fixed defense ratio for every account.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#units

Understand rapid fire before adding more fodder

Rapid fire can make a large number of cheap targets less protective than it looks. Test the actual attacker against the proposed mix. More fodder does not automatically improve the result when it gives the attacker favorable repeat shots.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#rapidfire

Shields protect their own unit

Shields belong to individual units and interact with shot size and combat rules. Do not pool all shield values into a single barrier. The reference separates the published threshold rule from unresolved exact-equality behavior.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#shields

Count permanent losses after reconstruction

Winning a battle and preserving investment are different results. Count losses after the applicable rebuilding chance, while recognizing that expected repair is not a guaranteed outcome. Restored defenses must also face any following wave.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#repair

Protect the bunker from missile preparation

ABMs can intercept IPMs, but the reserve is consumed. Missile preparation can remove defenses permanently before a fleet arrives. Do not assume a guaranteed mixed-defense spillover order or that a large Silo alone means enough interception stock.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#missiles

Use missile formulas as estimates, not launch guarantees

Weapons, Armour, interception and the selected defense target affect missile damage. Treat baseline formulas as estimates and validate the current launch. Range, travel time and damage are separate calculations.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#missile-math

Develop defense by measured need

Compare complete defense projects at a fixed resource budget, then build the smallest useful improvement. Include exposed resources, likely fleets and rebuilding reserves. A defense that wins an unrealistic test may still attract profitable routine raids.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#build-workflow

Make the simulator answer the real question

Hold the attacker and budget constant, then vary uncertain technology, loot, debris recovery and repeated waves. Distinguish defender survival from attacker profitability. Record the assumptions and permanent losses rather than only a green victory result.

Details and worked examples
https://theilluminate92.github.io/skie-guides/guide-defense-comparison-reference.html#simulation

When an attack is already coming

Read the incoming mission, timing and available resources before acting. Protect movable value and coordinate privately where useful. Panic spending or a blind missile response can add cost without changing the actual threat.

Quick checks:
- I know the stock and stationary assets my defense is intended to protect.
- I checked separate ship and defense debris settings, hostile speed and ACS.
- I tested realistic attackers with current research, lifeform bonuses and complete unit counts.
- I understand that domes have their own shields and rapid fire is probabilistic.
- I used post-repair losses and remaining counts when assessing follow-up waves.
- I checked ABM inventory and shared silo capacity, including protection for the moon.
- I have a fleetsave and collection routine instead of relying on a bunker for parked wealth.
