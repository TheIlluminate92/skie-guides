Use nanites for time and silos for a specific threat.
Skie Field Manual · Checked 8 November 2026. Construction examples are unmodified baselines; an archived missile flight-time discrepancy is explicitly flagged. Confirm displayed costs and timers in your universe.

This guide covers:
- Distinguish construction throughput from resource production.
- Compare a Nanite upgrade with the queues it will accelerate.
- Calculate a legal mix of Anti-Ballistic and Interplanetary Missiles.
- Check range, interception and irreversible launch consequences before firing.

Unlock the capability you actually need

Nanite Factories and Missile Silos are planet facilities. Build them where their effect is needed; a Nanite on one planet does not accelerate another planet or its moon. The following are standard base costs before applicable modifiers. Facility costs double with each successive level.

Capacity does not come preloaded
An empty Silo provides no interception. After unlocking ABMs, you must actually build them in the defense production menu.

Item | Requirements | Base cost: Metal / Crystal / Deuterium
Nanite Factory 1 | Robotics Factory 10 + Computer Technology 10 | 1,000,000 / 500,000 / 100,000
Missile Silo 1 | Shipyard 1 | 20,000 / 20,000 / 1,000
Anti-Ballistic Missile | Missile Silo 2 + Shipyard 1 | 8,000 / 0 / 2,000 each
Interplanetary Missile | Missile Silo 4 + Shipyard 1 + Impulse Drive 1 | 12,500 / 2,500 / 10,000 each

What a Nanite Factory accelerates

Each completed Nanite level halves the standard construction time of buildings, ships and defense at that planet. Relative to Nanite 0, levels 1, 2, 3 and 4 give factors of 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16. It does not directly increase mine output or speed ordinary research.

Robotics affects building construction; Shipyard affects ships and defense. Keep these roles distinct when diagnosing a slow queue. A higher Robotics level is not a substitute for Shipyard levels when producing a fleet.

Schedule a Nanite upgrade around local production. An active ship/defense queue can block the upgrade from starting, and Nanite construction blocks those production jobs. Adding an order to a queue is not the same as its being able to start. Finish needed ABMs or emergency ships before taking that production capacity offline.

Read a baseline construction calculation

For an ordinary building upgrade beyond the early-level speed-up, the baseline time in hours is (Metal + Crystal cost) ÷ [2,500 × (Robotics level + 1) × 2ᴺ × economy speed], where N is the already completed Nanite level. For ships and defense, use Shipyard level in place of Robotics. Deuterium cost is not part of this timing numerator.

The example assumes no Lifeform reductions, other modifiers or acceleration items. The game applies whole-second timing and minimum durations, so doubling a very fast facility is not necessarily useful for a unit already at the minimum. Use the displayed timer for the actual purchase.

Do not apply this blindly to Lifeforms
Lifeform buildings have their own scaling and modifiers. This table explains the classic construction factor; it is not a complete formula for every Lifeform building or technology.

Buy time when time is the bottleneck

1. List the next three construction or production jobs on the planet, their displayed durations and whether you can fund them.

2. Estimate how much of that schedule the next Nanite level saves. Include the Nanite’s own cost and the period during which its construction occupies or blocks local work.

3. Compare against the other bottleneck: if you wait days for resources and the queue is empty, faster production may not help immediately. If funded jobs are backed up, throughput has clear value.

4. Keep fuel, cargo replacement and essential defense reserves separate from the upgrade budget. Do not turn a convenience purchase into an unsafe transport situation.

5. When the upgrade completes, confirm its level and inspect the next job’s duration before filling a long queue. Record the result so that the next comparison uses your account’s actual modifiers.

A simple time comparison, not a resource-payback claim
Suppose the next funded work takes 48 hours at the current level. Halving it saves approximately 24 hours, but a Nanite upgrade occupying 30 hours would not make that short sequence finish earlier. A much longer funded backlog changes the answer. Earlier mine completion may also earn resources, which requires a separate production calculation.

Reserve capacity for both missile types

A Silo provides 10 capacity units per level. One ABM uses one unit and one IPM uses two: ABM count + 2 × IPM count must not exceed 10 × Silo level. Upgrading the Silo increases capacity, not missile range.

Choose a defensive reserve before filling the rest with offensive missiles. Once ABMs intercept an attack they are consumed, so remaining stock matters more than the Silo’s level. The planet’s ABMs also protect its moon against IPMs; you cannot build a separate Silo on the moon.

Do not demolish first and ask later
The archived official guide requires an empty Silo before demolition. Removing stored missiles is a separate destructive decision; upgrading the Silo is possible without emptying it. Inspect the current control and consequences before clearing anything.

Silo | Possible stock | Capacity used | What it demonstrates
2 | 20 ABMs | 20 / 20 | A small dedicated interception reserve
4 | 20 ABMs + 10 IPMs | 40 / 40 | Mixed defensive and offensive stock
5 | 10 ABMs + 20 IPMs | 50 / 50 | More offense means less interception reserve

Know what missiles destroy and what stops them

An ABM automatically destroys one incoming IPM and is itself consumed. Surviving IPMs attack defense, not the fleet or buildings. They ignore shields, and destroyed defenses do not receive the normal post-combat rebuilding chance. Attacker Weapons Technology and defender Armour Technology matter to the result.

Choose a primary defense type when launching. The aim is to remove a particular obstacle, not assume that one missile always destroys one turret. Use a current missile-capable simulator with the target’s technologies and defenses. Re-scout after impact before sending a fleet.

Interception comes before damage
If a planet has 12 ABMs and receives 20 IPMs, 12 missiles are intercepted and at most 8 reach the defenses, assuming no ABMs are added or consumed by another strike in the meantime. Those eight still need a damage calculation; “eight missiles” does not mean “eight Plasma Turrets destroyed”.

Separate missile range from travel time

The standard range is 5 × Impulse Drive level − 1 systems, within the same galaxy. Impulse 6 reaches 29 systems either way. Where circular solar systems are enabled, measure across the shorter wraparound route. Circular galaxies do not let an IPM cross into another galaxy.

The archived Formula Thread gives the baseline flight time as 30 + 60 × system distance seconds, divided by the relevant fleet-speed factor. The Origin universe-settings guide classifies missile attacks as war missions. Thus, ten systems at ×2 war speed give a baseline 315 seconds, or 5 minutes 15 seconds.

An archive inconsistency matters here
The archived IPM Guide instead prints 30 + 30 × distance. Because the official archives disagree, this lesson uses the Formula Thread baseline for explanation, not a guaranteed live countdown. Verify the game’s actual flight time before synchronising missiles with a fleet.

Run a final check before launch

1. Confirm the exact target, including planet versus moon, and obtain a recent report of defenses, ABMs and relevant technologies.

2. Check that the missile action is available from the intended source planet. Confirm range, stock, missile count and primary target.

3. Estimate the resource cost of intercepted missiles as well as those that reach defenses. Decide whether the resulting fleet operation still has a worthwhile objective.

4. Coordinate the timing if other strikes are involved, allowing for the defender rebuilding ABMs and for changes since your last report.

5. Read the final launch details carefully. IPMs cannot be recalled after launch. After impact, inspect the report and update the plan instead of repeating a salvo automatically.

A Silo is a tool, not a safety guarantee
ABMs do not stop attacking fleets or Moon Destruction missions. Keep your defense plan proportionate and continue fleetsaving; there is no building level that replaces supervision of valuable fleet returns.

Quick checks:
- I know which planet benefits from a Nanite upgrade and which queues it can block.
- My time estimate uses economy speed and the correct Robotics or Shipyard level.
- My intended missile mix fits ABMs + 2 × IPMs ≤ 10 × Silo level.
- I checked current ABM stock, range, target type and flight time.
- I understand that launching IPMs is irreversible and does not protect my fleet.
