Compare three different power sources

Solar Plants provide durable power without fuel. Satellites save fields but are vulnerable and temperature-dependent. Fusion offers dense, durable output at a Deuterium operating cost. The best mix depends on the demand, resources and infrastructure you already have.

Understand the scaling

Fusion output depends on reactor level and account-wide Energy Technology. Economy speed scales baseline fuel consumption, not energy output. Energy Technology does not raise Solar Plant or satellite output. Exact formulas and display-rounding assumptions are in the reference.

Why the same reactor becomes better

A shared Energy Technology upgrade can benefit several reactors, while a reactor upgrade helps one planet. At a common demand, compare total support costs and the resources retained after fuel. Spare power alone is not the financial benefit.

Switch in a controlled sequence

Keep existing production powered until replacement supply works. Compare net Deuterium and the next mine or crawler demand, retain recovery capacity, and verify any partial reactor setting in the resource screen. The reference preserves a transition checklist.

Details and worked examples

The decision rule

Prefer a system that meets useful demand with affordable construction, sustainable fuel and acceptable exposure. Existing solar, fusion and a modest satellite reserve can coexist. Do not demolish working infrastructure solely because a fresh-build calculation favors another option.
