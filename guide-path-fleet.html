GUIDE P3 / FLEET / GENERAL

Active-fleet play style

Choose targets and logistics before accumulating combat hulls.

9 MIN READ8 SECTIONSREVIEWED 2026-11-09

Play-style decisions reviewed 9 November 2026. Compare the current universe, class and active bonuses; examples are conditional planning comparisons.

Download text ↓

What this guide covers

  • Define the task a combat fleet must perform before buying ships.
  • Account for fuel, cargo, recovery and an exit plan in a raid decision.
  • Select class and Lifeform support for the ships and missions you really use.

Copy the selected text below.

SECTION 01

Decide what your fleet is for

An active fleet is an operating expense as well as an asset. Decide whether you want small profitable raids, organized alliance combat, debris collection or another specific activity. Those goals require different speed, cargo and fighting capability. General is the class to compare for combat-focused activity, but class bonuses do not turn an unsuitable fleet into a profitable one.

Begin with a role you can evaluate and a fleet you can afford to replace. Do not copy a fixed battleship-to-fighter ratio from an unrelated account. Enemy composition, technologies, defenses, universe settings and allied participation can change the result. A combat simulator is part of planning, not a license to assume a win.

SECTION 02

Make the support system reliable

  1. Learn fleetsaving and practise the route with a small fleet. Know where the main fleet will go when you are offline and how you will cover the return.
  2. Prepare enough Deuterium for the intended flight and the next safe move. A successful attack can still strand a fleet if you spend its last fuel.
  3. Provide cargo for the intended resource recovery and appropriate recycling capacity when debris collection is part of the plan. Do not count a debris field as recovered profit before it is collected.
  4. Use research and facilities to unlock the ships and handling capacity required for the chosen role. Do not fund extra combat hulls while basic fuel and transport are missing.

Defenses cannot protect an unattended valuable fleet

They can change an attacker’s calculation, but a sufficiently valuable target can justify a larger attack. The fleet’s movement and landing plan remain essential.

SECTION 03

Use a launch decision checklist

Read the most relevant intelligence you can obtain. Check what the report actually reveals and what remains unknown. Simulate with the technologies, ships, defenses and universe settings you can support. If a missing input could change the result, run a conservative case or do not take the fight. The simulation is only as reliable as the information entered.

Calculate expected usable loot and collectable debris, then subtract fuel and expected replacement losses in a consistent resource valuation. Allow for the possibility that the target moves, reinforcements arrive or someone else collects debris. Your original expected outcome is not guaranteed because the fleet has launched.

  1. Confirm the intended target, mission type, chosen fleet, speed and arrival time.
  2. Check that you can monitor the relevant flight and landing window and have a safe post-return plan.
  3. Review current rules governing attacks and allied coordination in your universe. Do not infer permission from another server’s settings.
  4. After dispatch, verify the event list and continue monitoring for material changes. If the situation no longer matches the plan, reassess rather than treating the original simulation as binding.
SECTION 04

Support the fleet you actually fly

Read the Lifeform technologies that apply to your chosen ship classes, fuel, speed, cargo or combat characteristics. Compare their benefit with normal combat or engine research and with simply buying the missing support ships. A technology for a ship you rarely use can be poor value despite an impressive percentage.

Mecha is a candidate to examine for fleet-oriented buildings, not a compulsory identity. Planet species, selected research and account class are separate layers. You can preserve an economic building setup while selecting a useful technology from another discovered species. Pilot changes on one suitable planet and record the actual bonus before repeating them across the account.

Research that changes speed can change your usual return schedule. Recheck travel times and fleetsave plans after upgrades. A stronger fleet with a return you cannot cover is not a safer fleet.

Check slot positions before assuming two useful technologies compete. Human Cruiser Mk II is in slot 10 and Mecha General Overhaul (Cruiser) is in slot 9, so a cruiser-focused plan can select both. They still compete with the other choices in their respective slots. Use the current Lifeforms tables for effective contributions and unlock costs instead of treating one ship focus as an automatic empire-wide recommendation.

SECTION 05

Review the operation, including the miss

Keep reports and lessons in an appropriate private place. Public guides do not need real coordinates, fleet schedules, names or account screenshots. Review the sequence: intelligence, decision, launch, monitoring, collection and safe return. Improve the weakest step before increasing the fleet’s value. Alliance cooperation details will live in their own section once its mechanics and operating procedures are confirmed.

A failed raid is still data

If the target moved and the fleet returned empty, record fuel spent, the observation you missed and whether the flight left other commitments exposed. Do not erase the result from the average. A routine with frequent empty flights can be less profitable than a smaller, better-qualified set of targets.

SECTION 06

Build a fleet for a repeatable job

The same ship can be excellent for one job and an expensive passenger in another. Grow the component that prevents a good operation from working: cargo, harvesting, scouting, speed, combat strength or fuel. Do not treat the largest possible fleet value as the objective by itself.

SECTION 07

Keep operating liquidity after buying hulls

Reserve Deuterium for the planned operation and the next safe move. Use the dispatch screen with the real composition, route and speed. Fuel reductions, class modifiers and server settings should be reflected in that number; do not subtract a remembered percentage again.

If a moon holds 1,000,000 Deuterium, a planned attack needs 600,000 and a safe post-return departure needs 500,000, the combined plan is 100,000 short. Expected loot is not a reliable substitute for fuel already in the right place. Replenish the moon or change the operation before committing.

Keep replacement and transport resources available as well. A win that leaves the account unable to move its other fleets can be a poor overall decision. Recycle only what you can actually collect, and do not spend predicted debris before the harvest result exists.

SECTION 08

Test the decision when the target changes

Use at least a normal case and an adverse case. The adverse case should reflect the uncertainty that matters: missing combat research, extra defenders, less loot, incomplete debris recovery or a changed arrival. A simulated victory with one optimistic input is not enough for an expensive launch.

If full debris recovery produces 4 million net but no recovery produces zero, the operation depends on harvesting. Treat recycling capacity and timing as a requirement, not a later convenience. If the target can move the loot and the remaining operation loses resources, decide in advance what evidence would make you recall while recall is still available.

Review empty flights and abandoned plans alongside successful hits. Useful metrics include net resources per supervised hour, fuel spent on misses, permanent ship losses and whether every return was handled safely. These are management choices, not reasons to publish sensitive coordinates or flight schedules.
